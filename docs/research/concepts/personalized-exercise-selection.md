# Personalized hypertrophy exercise selection

Nippard's *The Muscle Ladder* preorder exercise guide is a set of personal hypertrophy
preferences, not a universal ranking. The transferable principle is to **choose a movement
that fits the person's goal and equipment, feels comfortable, covers the intended muscle,
and remains measurable long enough to progress**. The guide's examples make this concrete:
machine chest press for hypertrophy if available and comfortable; a barbell or dumbbell
press may better fit a strength goal. A seated hamstring curl can complement a hip hinge,
and leg extensions can complement squats. See the [source notes](../raw/articles/2026-11-17-nippard-muscle-ladder-exercise-guide-notes.md).

## Selection order for a product

1. **Fit:** goal (hypertrophy vs strength/athletic performance), available equipment,
   tolerable range of motion, experience and technical demands. Pain is a reason to offer
   a different movement or range, never a cue to push through.
2. **Coverage:** compounds for demanding work, then isolation to address what that
   movement undertrains. Nippard's personal 50–70% compound ratio is not a universal target.
3. **Trackability:** retain a chosen exercise over enough sessions to compare reps,
   load and effort. Rotate deliberately when it stops fitting, not for random novelty.
4. **Useful tie-breaker:** tension at longer muscle lengths can favor a variant, but
   a single study or one coach's pick cannot establish an absolute “best” for every user.

## Mezo implications (ideas, not approved behavior)

- [Train](../../features/train.md) already has a 166-item catalog, plan generator,
  exercise swapping, per-exercise history, progression targets, and readiness/pain
  lightening. The new opportunity is a **reasoned substitute**: filter by equipment
  and user comfort, keep the intended muscle/function, show a short reason for
  the alternative, and keep the decision with the user.
- The plan review could explain **coverage gaps**, such as a knee-flexion hamstring
  exercise alongside a hip hinge, without imposing one preferred exercise or a fixed
  compound/isolation percentage.
- Exercise stories can explain why a variation may suit a goal and how to progress
  it. They should label coach preference separately from direct study evidence.
- Most named primary and backup movements already have catalog equivalents.
  Bayesian cable curl, direct forearm and neck work, and some exact variations
  need catalog review **only if** user demand justifies them; names alone do not
  justify adding exercises.
- Never encode the study percentages as predicted individual results. The
  triceps, hamstring and calf comparisons used small samples and fixed protocols
  ([triceps](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/),
  [hamstrings](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/),
  [calves](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38156065/)).

This complements the existing [program-design rules](program-design-rules.md)
and [set-volume landmarks](set-volume-landmarks.md); it does not replace their
volume, set or progression guidance.
