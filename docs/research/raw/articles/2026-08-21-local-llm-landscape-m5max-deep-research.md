# Local LLMs for Agentic Coding on a 128 GB M5 Max — State of Mid-2026 (researched 2026-08-21)

## 1. The user's model list — verified names and specs

**"Qwen 3.8 27B" → Qwen3.8-27B (real, very new).** Released ~Aug 14, 2026 by Alibaba, Apache 2.0. **Dense** 27B (~28B with vision encoder), 64 layers, **hybrid attention**: 48 Gated DeltaNet (linear-attention) layers : 16 full Gated Attention layers (3:1). Native **vision** (image/video input). Context **262,144 native, YaRN-extensible to ~1M**. Thinking mode on by default with "reasoning effort" levels (xhigh/medium/low) and Preserved Thinking across turns. Benchmarks from the HF model card: **SWE-bench Pro 61.7, Terminal-Bench 2.1 73.0, LiveCodeBench v6 90.3, GPQA-D 89.2**, plus computer-use scores (OSWorld 84.3). Recommended sampling: thinking temp 1.0/top_p 0.95/top_k 20. Sources: [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B), [QwenLM/Qwen3.8 GitHub](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8). One outlet reports it "within a percent" of 3.6-27B on speed ([InsiderLLM, updated 2026-08-16](https://insiderllm.com/guides/qwen-models-guide/)). The Qwen3.8 wave also includes a 2.4T-A95B "Max-class" checkpoint (custom license) — irrelevant for local. **Flag:** one week old at research time; MLX/llama.cpp/LM Studio support maturity for its hybrid stack could not be confirmed — historically the DeltaNet hybrids needed weeks for runtime support to stabilize.

**"Qwen 3.6 27B" → Qwen3.6-27B (real).** Released 2026-04-22, Apache 2.0. **Dense** 27B, same 3:1 DeltaNet/Gated-Attention hybrid (64 layers, hidden 5120). Context **262K native → 1.01M YaRN**. Thinking default on, Thinking Preservation. **SWE-bench Verified 77.2 ("best dense open coder" — beats the 15×-larger Qwen3.5-397B-A17B on agentic coding), Terminal-Bench 2.0 59.3, AIME 94.1**. Coding sampling: temp 0.6/top_p 0.95/top_k 20. Sources: [HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B), [QwenLM/Qwen3.6](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.6), [dev.to review](https://dev.to/czmilo/qwen36-35b-a3b-complete-review-alibabas-open-source-coding-model-that-beats-frontier-giants-4382).

**"Qwen 3.6 35B A3B" → Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (real).** Released 2026-04-16, Apache 2.0. **MoE**: 35B total / **3B active**, 40 layers, 256 experts (8 routed + 1 shared), same hybrid attention, **includes a vision encoder**. Context 262K → 1.01M. **SWE-bench Verified 73.4, Terminal-Bench 2.0 51.5, GPQA 86.0**. Source: [HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B). Trade: ~4 pts below the 27B dense on SWE-bench but ~5–10× faster decode (3B active).

**"Qwen 3 Coder Next" → Qwen3-Coder-Next (real).** Released 2026-02-03/04, Apache 2.0. **MoE 80B total / 3B active** (512 experts, 10+1 active), hybrid 48 layers (12× [3×DeltaNet-MoE + 1×GatedAttention-MoE]). **Non-thinking only** (no reasoning blocks — simpler for production agents). Context **262,144 native**. **SWE-bench Verified 70.6, SWE-bench Pro 44.3, Terminal-Bench 2.0 36.2**; trained on 800K executable tasks with environment-interaction RL; explicitly tuned for long-horizon tool use and recovery from execution failures. Sampling: temp 1.0/top_p 0.95/top_k 40. Sources: [HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Coder-Next), [Unsloth guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3-coder-next), [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/qwen/qwen3-coder-next), [tech report](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00729v1). Note the newer Qwen3.6-27B beats it on SWE-bench V (77.2 vs 70.6), but Coder-Next remains the tool-calling/agent-harness favorite and much faster (3B active). No "Coder" variant of 3.6/3.8 has shipped yet.

**"Gemma 4" (real).** Google DeepMind, released ~2026-04 (model card last updated 2026-07-30), open weights. Five sizes: **E2B, E4B (edge, 128K ctx), 12B dense (256K), 26B-A4B MoE (25.2B total/3.8B active, 256K), 31B dense (30.7B, 256K)**. Interleaved local sliding-window + global attention; multimodal (text+image; audio on ≤12B); **native function calling**. 31B: **LiveCodeBench v6 80.0, τ²-bench retail 86.4 / avg 76.9, AIME 89.2, Codeforces ELO 2150**; 26B-A4B: LCB 77.1, τ² 68.2. Sources: [official model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4), [LM Studio catalog](https://lmstudio.ai/models/gemma-4). **Flag:** no first-party SWE-bench Verified number found on the model card — third-party "Gemma 4 SWE-bench" pages exist but are SEO-grade; community consensus is Gemma 4 is a strong generalist/tool-caller but not a SWE-bench leader vs the Qwen coder line.

**"Hermes 4 70B" (real but dated).** Nous Research, released **2025-08-26** — a year old now. Built on **Meta-Llama-3.1-70B** (dense), hybrid reasoning (optional reasoning traces), **131,072 context**, good steerability/low refusals, JSON-mode/function calling. Benchmark standing in 2026 is weak for coding: MMLU-Pro 66.4, GPQA 49.1, "Intelligence index" 19th percentile ([galaxy.ai model page](https://blog.galaxy.ai/model/hermes-4-70b), [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/nousresearch/hermes-4-70b)). The newest Nous *model* is **Hermes 4.3 (36B, ByteDance Seed base, 2025-08-25)**; through 2026 Nous pivoted to the **Hermes Agent framework** (Hermes Desktop June 2026, "Herald" release 2026-08-03) rather than new large checkpoints — **no Hermes 5 exists as of Aug 2026** ([hermes-ai.net/news](https://hermes-ai.net/news/), [TechCrunch 2026-07-13](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/13/hermes-agent-maker-nous-research-in-talks-for-new-funding-at-1-5b-valuation/)). **Verdict for this use case:** Hermes 4 70B is outclassed for agentic coding by every Qwen model above, and as a dense Llama-3 70B it's also the slowest option on Apple Silicon. Its niche is persona/steerability, not SWE work.

## 2. What the community actually runs on 128 GB Macs (mid-2026)

- **Qwen3-Coder-Next 80B-A3B** — the default "serious local coding agent" pick for 128 GB Macs: frontier-adjacent agentic coding, 3B active = fast decode, fits with huge context headroom ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3-coder-next), [KDnuggets top-7](https://www.kdnuggets.com/top-7-coding-models-you-can-run-locally-in-2026)).
- **Qwen3.6-27B dense** — best per-parameter SWE-bench model; also the model LM Studio itself benchmarks its agentic MLX engine with ([LM Studio blog, 2026-06-05](https://lmstudio.ai/blog/mlx-engine-agentic-workloads)).
- **MiniMax-M2.7** (succeeds M2.5; 230B-A10B, ~200K ctx, SWE-Pro 56.2, Terminal-Bench 2 57.0): the biggest thing that genuinely fits — Unsloth's **UD-IQ4_XS dynamic 4-bit is 108 GB and runs at ~15+ tok/s on a 128 GB Mac** ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/tutorials/minimax-m27), [LM Studio catalog](https://lmstudio.ai/models/minimax/minimax-m2.7); MiniMax tool-call format natively supported since LM Studio 0.3.31). Strongest quality ceiling locally, but tight: ~15 GB left for KV + OS.
- **gpt-oss-120b** (117B MoE, MXFP4, 128K ctx, Apache 2.0) — still popular; strong reasoning+tool calling, ~60–65 GB footprint leaves lots of context room; community reports occasional tool-call flakiness in coding harnesses ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-oss/), [BenchLM Aug 2026](https://benchlm.ai/models/gpt-oss-120b), [GitLab issue on flakiness](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab/-/issues/579334)).
- **GLM-4.7-Flash** (Zhipu, Jan 2026, 30B-A3B MoE, ~128–200K ctx, SWE-bench V 59.2) — the budget/speed tier king, but on 128 GB you can do better ([MarkTechPost 2026-01-20](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/01/20/zhipu-ai-releases-glm-4-7-flash-a-30b-a3b-moe-model-for-efficient-local-coding-and-agents/)). Full **GLM-4.7 (358B)** and **GLM-5.1 (754B-A40B)** do NOT fit 128 GB; **Kimi K3 (2.8T)** is API-only territory.
- **Gemma 4 31B / 26B-A4B** — best local *tool-use generalist* (τ²-bench), good for plan/spec writing and review; not the SWE-bench leader.
- **Flag:** direct r/LocalLLaMA threads could not be surfaced through search (results were blogs/aggregators, several SEO-grade — e.g. "Laguna XS 2.1" and "Nemotron 3 Super" appeared only in low-trust listicles and are **unverified**). The convergent picture across Unsloth/LM Studio/HF/KDnuggets: **Coder-Next or Qwen3.6-27B as daily driver, MiniMax-M2.7 as the quality ceiling, gpt-oss-120b as reasoning alternate**.

## 3. MLX vs GGUF on Apple Silicon (2026)

- **Generation speed:** MLX is generally **15–25% faster** than llama.cpp/GGUF on the same model/quant; larger gaps (up to ~3×) reported against the *legacy* llama.cpp Metal backend before its 2025/26 rework ([yage.ai deep-dive, 2026-03-31](https://yage.ai/share/mlx-apple-silicon-en-20260331.html), [codersera comparison](https://codersera.com/blog/ollama-vs-lm-studio-vs-vllm-vs-llama-cpp-vs-mlx-2026/)).
- **Long-context prefill:** measured on Qwen3-Next-80B, 128 GB Mac Studio: MLX 8-bit beat GGUF Q4_K_M by ~15% at 30K–60K prompts (60K: 71.6s vs 84.2s); roughly tied at 12K ([QwenLM discussion #139](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.6/discussions/139)). On **M5 specifically, MLX exploits the new GPU Neural Accelerators; llama.cpp support lagged** — this makes MLX the clear choice on M5-class hardware.
- **The decisive agentic factor is caching, not raw speed.** LM Studio's **mlx-engine v1.8.5 (2026-06-05)** added **disk-backed KV-cache checkpointing at 256-token boundaries + continuous batching**, built specifically for agent loops that rewind/extend prompts (and for hybrid/SWA models like Qwen 3.5/3.6 and Gemma 4 whose caches can't be trivially rewound). Benchmarked on Qwen3.6-27B-MLX-4bit: 2.2× throughput at 4 concurrent requests, **82% less extra RAM on 33K-token prompts** ([LM Studio blog](https://lmstudio.ai/blog/mlx-engine-agentic-workloads)). llama.cpp's `llama-server` has its own strong prompt-cache story (80× warm-cache speedup on 60K-token re-prompts in the discussion above), and multi-slot serving.
- **Quality parity:** MLX 4-bit RTN ≈ GGUF Q4 in practice; at matched large-model configs Q4_K_M and MLX-6bit score "virtually identical" ([Presenc benchmarks](https://presenc.ai/research/local-llm-quantization-quality-benchmarks-2026)). But note: MLX default conversion is plain round-to-nearest — an **imatrix-calibrated GGUF or an MLX DWQ/AWQ quant beats a naive MLX RTN quant at 4-bit** ([D-Central format guide](https://d-central.tech/llm-quantization-formats/)).
- **LM Studio** supports both engines with auto-switching; on Apple Silicon prefer MLX builds when a well-made quant exists, GGUF (Unsloth UD dynamic quants) when it doesn't.

## 4. Quantization guidance (RAM is not the constraint at 128 GB)

- **Consensus for coding/agentic in 2026: 4-bit is a floor, not a target.** Q4_K_M ≈ 1.5–3% benchmark degradation but the damage concentrates in exactly this workload: structured output, multi-step reasoning, long agent loops. **Q6_K ≈ 0.5–1.5%, rarely observable; Q8_0 ≈ negligible** ([SitePoint framework](https://www.sitepoint.com/q4-vs-q6-vs-q8-quantization-local-llms/), [runlocalmodel](https://runlocalmodel.com/choosing-quantization-2026.html)).
- **Direct evidence on agentic reliability:** a documented Qwen3.6 agent deployment saw recurring tool-call/format errors at Q4_K_M drop to "multi-day interval" after switching to Q6 ([note.com case study](https://note.com/samehadaonsen/n/n2a94f796aefa?hl=en)); Unsloth recommends **Q4_K_XL/Q6_K_XL "for frequent tool calls" because they keep attention layers at higher precision**. Small-active-parameter MoEs (3B active) are more quant-sensitive than dense models — prefer 6-bit+ or Unsloth dynamic (UD) quants that protect router/attention layers.
- **Concrete picks:** Qwen3.6-27B / Qwen3.8-27B / Gemma 4 31B → **MLX 8-bit or Q8_0 (~28–33 GB)** — trivial fit, no reason to go lower. Qwen3-Coder-Next 80B → **Q6_K/MLX-6bit (~66 GB)** or Q8_0 (~85 GB), both fit with big context. MiniMax-M2.7 → forced to **UD-IQ4_XS (108 GB)**; the only model where 4-bit is accepted. Hermes/70B dense → Q6_K ~58 GB fits easily, but see throughput below.
- **Formats:** AWQ/GPTQ are GPU-server formats — irrelevant on Mac. **MLX DWQ** (distillation-calibrated) is the best-quality MLX low-bit option; Apple's own mixed-precision trick (6-bit embeddings/output + 4-bit body) is common in community MLX quants. KV-cache quantization (mlx-lm: 4/8-bit since late 2024) — use **8-bit KV** for agentic work; 4-bit KV has anecdotal tool-call degradation (unverified quantitatively).

## 5. Realistic throughput on M5 Max (128 GB, ~600 GB/s)

Generation (decode) is bandwidth-bound; M5 Max ≈ **+28% over M4 Max**. Community/aggregated MLX numbers ([llmcheck](https://llmcheck.net/blog/apple-silicon-m5-max-local-ai-guide/), [Apple MLX/M5 post, 2025-11-19](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/exploring-llms-mlx-m5)):

| Model class | M5 Max gen speed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 8B Q4 | ~82 tok/s | |
| 30–35B-A3B MoE Q4 | ~58 tok/s (M4 Max ~45) | Qwen 3.5-30B-A3B figure |
| 27–31B dense Q4 | ~25–35 tok/s (est.) | no direct measurement found — **flag: extrapolated** from bandwidth ÷ weight size |
| 80B-A3B MoE Q4–Q6 | ~40–50 tok/s (est.); "feels like a 4B model" | M4-class 128 GB measurement context below |
| 70B dense Q4 | ~18 tok/s (M4 Max 14; Q5 15–20) | Q6/Q8 proportionally slower (~12/9) |
| MiniMax-M2.7 IQ4_XS | ~15+ tok/s | Unsloth figure, 128 GB Mac |

**Prefill (the agentic bottleneck):** M5's GPU Neural Accelerators give **3.3–4.1× faster time-to-first-token vs M4** under MLX (Apple-measured on Qwen 1.7B–30B-MoE; compute-bound so it scales to Max chips). Real long-context numbers on a 128 GB Mac Studio for Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B: **cold prefill 12K→~12s, 30K→~29s (MLX 8-bit), 60K→~72–84s, 120K→~250s (GGUF)**; with prompt caching, re-processing a 60K context drops to **~1s** ([QwenLM discussion #139](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.6/discussions/139) — **flag:** that thread says "M4 Ultra"; verify chip designation, may be M3 Ultra-class ~800 GB/s, i.e. an M5 Max would be somewhat slower cold but identical warm-cache behavior).

**Verdict:** a 30B-class hybrid dense or 80B-A3B MoE is comfortably interactive for agentic coding (fast decode + M5 prefill accelerators + KV checkpointing). A **dense 70B is not**: ~18 tok/s decode and multi-minute cold prefills at 100K context make it a poor agent backbone in 2026.

## 6. Context windows and KV-cache reality

- **Native contexts:** Qwen3.6/3.8 (27B, 35B-A3B) and Qwen3-Coder-Next: **262,144 native**, YaRN → ~1M. Gemma 4 medium: **256K native**. gpt-oss-120b: 128K. MiniMax-M2.7: ~196K. Hermes 4 70B: 131K.
- **KV cost is now architecture-dependent, and the Qwen hybrids are the whole game.** With 3/4 of layers as constant-state DeltaNet + SWA on the rest, Qwen3-Next-class models cost **~25 GB of KV per 1M tokens (~4× less than a pure transformer)** — i.e., **128K–260K contexts cost only ~3–7 GB** ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2025-09-11-qwen3-next), [discussion #139](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.6/discussions/139): Q4_K_M 80B + 4×250K slots = ~110 GB total). Gemma 4's interleaved SWA is similarly cheap. Contrast a classic dense-GQA 70B (Llama-3.1/Hermes 4): ~0.31 MB/token fp16 → **~40 GB at 128K** (half that with 8-bit KV) — computed estimate, not benchmarked.
- **Practical usefulness:** advertised vs *effective* context remain distinct — models still score best at short contexts and degrade non-uniformly toward their limits ([RULER-style 2026 evals](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07731)). The Qwen 3.6+ "Thinking Preservation" mechanism plus LM Studio's KV checkpointing were built precisely because agent sessions routinely exceed 100K; community experience says 128K-scale sessions are genuinely workable on the hybrid Qwen models, while pushing to the YaRN-extended 1M is not worth it for coding quality. Caveat: **Qwen3-Coder-Next's own card recommends 32K deployment ctx "to avoid OOM" on GPU servers — irrelevant on a 128 GB unified-memory Mac**, where 256K fits.

## Bottom line

1. **Daily agentic-coding driver:** Qwen3-Coder-Next 80B-A3B at MLX-6bit/Q6_K (tool-native, fast, 256K) or Qwen3.8-27B / Qwen3.6-27B at 8-bit (highest SWE quality per token, thinking model — better for plan/spec writing and review). 2. **Quality ceiling:** MiniMax-M2.7 UD-IQ4_XS when 15 tok/s is acceptable. 3. **Skip:** Hermes 4 70B (dated, dense-70B-slow, weak coding). Gemma 4 31B is a fine reviewer/generalist but not the coder. 4. Run **MLX in LM Studio (≥ mlx-engine 1.8.5)** with 8-bit KV cache, 6–8-bit weights — never 4-bit for tool-calling work when RAM allows.

**Unverified/low-confidence items:** dense-27B M5 Max tok/s (extrapolated); "M4 Ultra" chip label in the Qwen throughput thread; Gemma 4 SWE-bench Verified (no first-party number); "Laguna XS 2.1"/"Nemotron 3 Super" (SEO-only mentions); direct r/LocalLLaMA thread sentiment (search returned aggregators, not threads).
