# Reading notes (independent paraphrase, not a copy of the bonus PDF)

- pp. 2–3: These are Nippard's personal favorites for hypertrophy, not a universal exercise ranking or a ready-made program. Goal, equipment, anatomy, injury history, comfort, and ability to progress can change the best choice. He favors systematic variation over frequent random rotation. A compound plus isolation mix is useful; his own 50–70% compound share is an example, not a prescription.
- p. 3: His three selection tests are meaningful tension in a stretched muscle position, repeatable progression in load or reps, and a pain-free comfortable range of motion. Technically demanding compounds generally go earlier; isolation fills gaps.
- pp. 4–14: Upper-body primary choices: machine chest press; cable lateral raise; close neutral-grip pulldown; chest-supported T-bar row; Bayesian cable curl; overhead cable triceps extension; wrist curl plus reverse curl; neck curl plus extension; trap-bar shrug; cable crunch. Alternatives include incline dumbbell press, leaned dumbbell lateral raise, neutral-grip pull-up, deficit Pendlay row, preacher curl, skull crusher, wrist roller, wide-grip shrug, and weighted crunch.
- pp. 15–22: Lower-body primary choices: good morning, hack squat, lunge, seated leg curl, standing calf raise. Alternatives: deadlift variants, barbell back/front squat, hip thrust, Nordic curl or Romanian deadlift, leg-press calf raise.
- pp. 18–21: Squats and hack squats leave some rectus femoris work to leg extensions; a hip hinge does not train the biceps femoris short head in the same way as a knee-flexion curl. These are useful examples of covering a gap, not reasons to reject the compound lift.
- p. 22: Lengthened partial calf raises are presented as an optional experiment, grounded in a calf-specific trial; do not generalize to all muscles/exercises.
- p. 23 references 1–11: the PDF cites a systematic review on exercise variation and studies of long-muscle-length training, proximity to failure, overhead triceps extensions, seated leg curls, standing calf raises, and calf partials. The exact percentage comparisons come from small studies and should not become user promises.

Primary-source checks performed 2026-11-17:

- Exercise variation review, 8 studies / 241 young men: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/
- Overhead vs neutral triceps extension, 21 adults: whole triceps volume +19.9% vs +13.9% in the studied 12-week protocol: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/
- Seated vs prone leg curl, 20 adults: whole hamstrings +14% vs +9% in the studied 12-week protocol: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/
- Standing vs seated calf raise, 14 untrained adults: whole triceps surae +5.6% vs +2.1%; soleus difference was not significant: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38156065/
- Lengthened partial calf raise trial: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/
